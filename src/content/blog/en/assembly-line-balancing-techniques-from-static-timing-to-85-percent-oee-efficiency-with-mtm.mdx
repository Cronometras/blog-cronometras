# Assembly Line Balancing Techniques: From Static Timing to >85% OEE Efficiency with MTM

In the current Spanish industrial scenario, marked by transition towards Industry 4.0, line balancing based on simple arithmetic averages is one of the common causes of **OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) stagnation**. Many manufacturing plants have assembly lines with double-digit productivity improvement potential simply by applying modern balancing techniques (illustrative example: 20-40%).

Modern plant engineering faces a fundamental problem: "theoretical efficiency" in Excel does not support plant reality. Human variability ($\sigma$) and increasingly strict ergonomic restrictions (ISO 11228) force abandoning basic heuristic. To reach real efficiencies exceeding 85%, it is imperative to transition from reactive timing to **Scientific Methods Engineering**. Two valid families of methods exist: sector predetermined time systems (MTM/MOST) and stopwatch time study with synthetic pace rating (apreciación sintética del ritmo) and ILO/TAL allowances —the **Cronometras** approach—, which reaches equally defensible standard times by rating the operator's pace.

## The Anatomy of Imbalance: Why Theoretical Takt Time Fails

Most common error in production management is assuming cycle time is a deterministic constant. A large share of line imbalances (illustrative example: 68%) does not come from poor task assignment, but from uncontrolled standard deviations in human work cycles.

### The Average Error
Using simple timing without rigorous pace valuation (Rating/Performance Factor) generates false data. If a line is balanced using arithmetic average of 10 timing samples without normalizing, operator inefficiency is integrated into process standard.

### Theoretical Takt Time vs. Operational Takt Time
For detailed engineering, classic formula ($Time / Demand$) is insufficient. A robust balance must be calculated against **Operational Takt Time**, integrating projected availability and performance losses.

$$Takt_{Op} = \frac{\text{Available Time} \times OEE_{target}}{\text{Demand}}$$

Ignoring OEE factor in this equation and balancing against maximum theoretical capacity generates **Muri (Overburden)**: line is saturated with a load that, at first micro-stop or method variation, collapses flow, creating floating bottlenecks impossible to trace.

## Measurement Methodologies: Stopwatch vs. MTM/MOST

Choice of measurement approach shapes the line's productivity ceiling. Two valid families of methods exist.

### Stopwatch Time Study with Synthetic Pace Rating
The **Cronometras** approach observes real work with a stopwatch, applies synthetic pace rating (apreciación sintética del ritmo) and ILO/TAL allowances. It is the more agile and simple system to deploy, although it requires trained analysts. Two effects must be controlled: the **Hawthorne Effect** (the operator modifies behavior when observed) and the calibration of pace judgment.

### MTM and MOST: Sector Predetermined Time Systems
Predetermined Time Systems (PMTS) like MTM-2, MTM-UAS, or MOST are sector methods with three notable strengths:

1.  **Pre-Process Validation:** Allows balancing line before physically building it or manufacturing prototypes, based on workstation design.
2.  **TMU Granularity:** PMTS work in *Time Measurement Units* (1 TMU = 0.036 seconds). This allows dissecting cycle into basic motions (Reach, Grasp, Turn), surfacing hidden times a global reading misses.
3.  **Method-Based Standard:** They define what the process *should* take under a given ideal method.

**Balancing Efficiency (BE):** Both families, correctly applied by trained analysts, deliver comparable balance efficiency; the choice depends on the cycle characteristics and the data available.

## Hard Constraints: Normative Ergonomics (ISO 11228) and Fatigue

For 2025, adaptation to European regulation on **Musculoskeletal Disorders (MSD)** is a hard limiting factor. A balance saturating operator at 98% of Takt Time is technically unfeasible if physiological recovery is not considered.

### Allowance Calculation (Rest Coefficients)
Modern time engineering must mathematically integrate rest coefficients within work cycle. This is not optional; it is an **ILO** requirement and prevention standard.

### Sustainable Pace
**Cronometras** goal is not speed, but sustainable normal pace. Technically we define this as **100 BS (British Scale)** or **60 Bedaux**. Any balance requiring performance superior to this standard continuously will result in accumulated fatigue, quality drop, and absenteeism, destroying OEE long term.

## Smoothing Algorithms and Bottleneck Management

Once standard times are debugged, challenge is assignment algorithm.

### Real Bottleneck Identification
In dynamic environment, bottleneck is not necessarily slowest station on average, but **station with highest variance ($\sigma^2$)**. Here a sector PMTS such as MOST, or a stopwatch study with pace rating, standardizes the method and reduces dispersion.

### Minimizing Smoothness Index
We use **Ranked Positional Weight** algorithms for work element reassignment respecting precedences. Mathematical goal is minimizing *Smoothness Index* (SI), reducing total system idle time:

$$SI = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (Cycle_{max} - Cycle_{i})^2}$$

To achieve SI close to zero, **breakdown into minimal elements** is indispensable. We don't balance "tasks" (e.g. "Mount Pump"), we balance "motions" (e.g. "Position P1SE + Screw T1"), allowing micro-adjustments of load between stations.

### Technical FAQ

**How does rest coefficient affect final line OEE?**
If not calculated correctly within cycle time, rest coefficient will appear as inexplicable "performance loss" in OEE. By integrating it into standard (allowed time), OEE reflects operational reality and allows planning real capacity.

**Is applying MOST profitable in short series or only mass production?**
Historically reserved for massive. Today, with MOST flexibility (BasicMOST or MiniMOST version), it is highly profitable in medium and short series where manual method variability is biggest cost-driver.

**What is implementation cost difference between heuristic and scientific balance?**
Initial MTM engineering cost is higher (requires certified analysts), but operational cost reduces drastically. Heuristic balance usually carries hidden inefficiency (direct labor cost) throughout project lifespan.
