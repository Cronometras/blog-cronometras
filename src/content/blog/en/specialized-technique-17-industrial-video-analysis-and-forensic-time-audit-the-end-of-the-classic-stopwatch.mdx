## From "Hawthorne Effect" to Digital Precision: Why Evolve?

Traditional timing (whether snapback or cumulative) suffers from insurmountable physical limitations for the human analyst. The main barrier is physiological: the human eye has critical difficulties capturing and segmenting micro-movements in high-frequency cycles, specifically those lasting only a couple of seconds (illustrative example).

### The End of Subjectivity in Performance Rating
One of the historical friction points between engineering and labor is Performance Rating (Pacing). In an in-situ observation, the assessment is ephemeral and subjective.
Technique #17 eliminates parallax error and, more importantly, stabilizes activity judgment. Through video analysis, the pace rating can be reviewed as many times as needed before it is signed off, which reduces rating subjectivity (illustrative example: rating deviation below ±2%).

> **Technical Fact:** Transitioning to video systems eliminates the "Hawthorne Effect" (alteration of operator behavior when feeling observed), as POV action cameras or fixed cameras are less invasive than an analyst "breathing down the neck" of the worker.

## Technical Integration: Video-Chronometry and Predetermined Time Systems (PMTS)

Video analysis also pairs well with sector predetermined time systems like **MTM-1, MTM-2, and MOST**.

### *Frame-by-Frame* Analysis and Therbligs
Coding each movement element requires surgical precision. A complex "Grasp" movement can last milliseconds.
*   **Precision:** By working with **60 fps** recordings, we obtain a temporal resolution of 0.016 seconds per frame. This allows detecting the exact start and end of each *Therblig*, validating codes impossible to audit in real-time.
*   **NVA (Non-Value Added) Detection:** Video analysis makes non-value-added activities easier to spot than direct observation. The human eye tends to "smooth out" micro-waits; video is relentless.

### Traceability Across the Analysis Phases
At **Cronometras**, the video is analysed frame by frame and each observation is tied to the filmed fragment, which creates total traceability. There are no "black boxes"; every second of standard time is backed by visual evidence.

## Legal and Regulatory Framework in Spain

For Plant Managers and HR Directors, implementing cameras usually raises privacy alerts. However, under **Technique #17**, the approach is strictly technical, not disciplinary.

### GDPR and Proportionality Principle
Recording images of workers requires prior information and compliance with the applicable data protection regulation (GDPR in the European Union, alongside Spain's LOPD-GDD). The proportionality principle applies: recording must be a suitable and necessary measure for process engineering, a legitimate company purpose.
*   **Legal Distinction:** Differentiating between disciplinary surveillance and methods study is critical.
*   **Union Management (Art. 20.3 ET):** These practices require prior information to the people concerned and to workers' representatives. In a Cronometras study the goal is stated up front: method improvement and ergonomics, not punitive control.

### Algorithmic Transparency and Rider Law
Looking towards 2025, EU directives on AI will demand transparency if times feed productivity algorithms. Video analysis offers necessary "explainability": the standard is not dictated by an opaque algorithm, but by a visually auditable measurement.

## Direct Impact on OEE and Technical Productivity

Applying forensic time audit directly impacts the operating income statement.

1.  **Reduction of Performance Losses:** Video reveals invisible micro-stops silently eroding OEE.
2.  **SMED Efficiency:** Video analysis can reveal further reduction opportunities in changeover times. The reason? It allows identifying and externalizing operator movements performed outside the machine zone that the in-situ observer usually ignores by focusing on tooling.
3.  **Engineering Efficiency:** Decoupling data collection from analysis reduces engineer time. Capture (Phase 1) can be done by a technician on site, while the analyst assesses and codes the footage (Phase 2) without repeated plant-floor visits.
